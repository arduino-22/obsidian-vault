### 1. The "70 Points" Myth
You correctly identified that passing with 70 points is technically possible, but it is not a guarantee. The biggest risk is forgetting something crucial, which would be devastating if your VPN disconnects right before the exam.

### 2. Documenting is Your Secret Weapon
This is the most critical piece of advice. You mentioned that you didn't discuss Metasploit in your report because it was too risky. **This is a great point to make.** In a certification exam, documentation is often the only way to prove you passed.
*   **Actionable Tip:** Start taking screenshots of key moments (shell gain, proof files) immediately. This creates a "digital audit trail" that you can rely on if you are ever tested again.

### 3. Prioritize Lab Time Over Course Text
You are absolutely right about this. The OSCP is a hands-on test, and the course materials can feel like a barrier.
*   **Actionable Tip:** Treat the lab time as your only "reference." Don't look up concepts while you are working. Focus all your energy on the machines you actually enumerated.

### 4. Brute Force vs. Automation
The OSCP is not just about knowing commands; it's about knowing *when* to use them.
*   **Actionable Tip:** Practice the "decision tree" for your own exam.
    *   **Brute Force:** Use it when you are stuck on a single machine for 20 minutes or more.
    *   **Automation:** Use scripts to find a whole network segment or a specific file type, then stop there. You don't need to be a super-hacker; you just need to know the "why" behind the "how."

### 5. Practice Exams are Essential
You mentioned that reading "OSCP Journey" posts is overwhelming. The solution is to practice under real constraints.
*   **Actionable Tip:** Do a full-length practice exam *after* you have reviewed your own report. This will force you to apply the "try harder" mindset and the documentation habits you just learned.

### 6. Don't Be Afraid to Use Metasploit
You wisely chose not to discuss it in your report, but you are right that it is a tool.
*   **Actionable Tip:** If you are still learning Metasploit, use it. But always do a quick check on the MD5 hash of your report *before* submitting it. It’s a safety net.

### 7. Focus on Transferable Skills
The technical skills (password cracking, reporting) are transferable to most security roles. The "try harder" attitude and threat modeling are universal human traits.
*   **Actionable Tip:** If you are looking for a path, look into Threat Modeling or Security Consulting—these are in high demand and rely on the same analytical skills you will gain from OSCP.

### 8. The "HTB" List is a Tool, Not a Target
The TJNull HTB machines list is a great resource for networking practice.
*   **Actionable Tip:** Use it to practice network pivots (moving between networks), but don't let it become your primary focus.
